How I Report

The standards I use for work published in this portfolio are informed by established newsroom ethics and reporting practices.

✓

Accuracy & Verification

Accuracy takes priority over speed. Consequential or disputed facts should be checked against primary records, first-hand reporting, or independent sources when appropriate. Unverified information is attributed and uncertainty is made clear.

Documents, datasets, photographs, audio, video and online material are evidence to be checked, not assumed to be authentic or complete. Whenever possible, reporting traces information back to the original record.

Authenticity

Reporting should reflect reality as faithfully as possible. Events are not staged, scenes or quotations are not fabricated, and a source's meaning is not altered.

Synthetic, reconstructed or materially altered images, audio or video are never presented as documentary evidence and are clearly identified when used.

Fairness & Right of Reply

Fairness means including significant facts and representing relevant perspectives in proportion to what the evidence supports, not simply giving every position equal weight.

People and institutions facing serious criticism or unfavorable factual allegations should have a meaningful opportunity to respond before publication.

Sourcing & Attribution

Named Sources First

On-the-record, identifiable sources are preferred so readers can judge where information comes from and why it may be credible.

Anonymous Sources

Anonymity is exceptional: the information should be important, unavailable on the record, and supplied by someone in a position to know it.

Clear Attribution

Disputed claims are attributed, and reporting from other outlets is credited and checked against the underlying evidence when available.

Interviews & Quotations

Quotations should preserve a source's words, context and intended meaning. If a quote is unclear, paraphrasing is preferable to rewriting what was said.

Sources do not approve finished copy. Specific technical details may be checked for accuracy without giving a source control over framing, conclusions or publication.

Independence & Transparency

Reporting should remain independent of political, financial, personal or organizational pressure. Relevant conflicts are avoided where possible and disclosed when they cannot reasonably be avoided.

Documented fact is distinguished from analysis, inference and opinion. Methods, limitations and material uncertainties are disclosed when they affect how a reader should understand the work.

Corrections

Factual errors should be corrected promptly and visibly. A substantive correction should state what was wrong and provide the accurate information rather than silently replacing the original claim.

Corrections: If you believe a piece contains a factual error, email MayaChelle.academic@gmail.com with the passage and supporting source. Substantive corrections will be noted on the project page.

AI Use

AI may be used for limited production assistance such as formatting or organizing material. It does not replace reporting, sourcing, verification or editorial judgment. Factual material is checked against reliable sources, and meaningful AI use is disclosed on the individual article or project.
